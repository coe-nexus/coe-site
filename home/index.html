The story

A few years before he died, my brother gave me his last five hundred dollars.

Tolik walked to work that whole winter so I could go to college. Northeastern winters. He ate once a day and never said a word about it. He just gave everything he had, quietly, the way he did most things.

I went on to Harvard. Then Google, then SAP. I built learning programs that reached millions of people. I told myself I was making good on what he gave me.

What I did not see was what he was carrying. A traumatic brain injury. Headaches that made him scream. A fog and an invisible pain that I was too busy climbing to recognize.

In 2014 he sent me three words. "Need to talk."

"Need to talk."
"at conference. talk later?"
"ok."

Two letters. They sat in my chest for ten years.

He was gone two days later. He was thirty.

Then, in a moment of deep forgiveness, he came to me.

Not in a dream. In something I can only describe as an awakening, the kind that rearranges what you thought you understood about love and sacrifice and what a life is for.

He was not my brother who left me. He was my soldier who finished his mission. And the mission did not end with him. It became Covenant of Education, the work I do now in his name, for the kids whose pain no one around them can see.

About Covenant of Education

Covenant of Education is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. We teach teenagers to find self acceptance, then deeper meaning in their life through a framework called the 7 Teachings, the same framework that pulled me out of a self-destructive cycle after Tolik's death, and gave me the strength and vision to start COE.

The work we are walking toward: one hundred million people discovering deeper purpose in their life by 2040. Membership is simply how people choose to walk that path to self discovery with our community.
